# Pacing and rhythm

## Measured rhythm (24 refs)

| Metric | Value |
|---|---|
| Typical length | 7–17 s (median ≈ 11.5 s); long-form 24–37 s |
| Significant motion events | 0.45–0.95 per second (median ≈ 0.65/s, i.e. **a big move every ~1.5 s**) |
| Median gap between big motion peaks | 20–50 f |
| Small events (a word, a pop, a number tick) | every 4–12 f; there is always something |
| Hard cuts in the body | 0–3 typical; fast "montage" bursts of 4 cuts in 1 s before the logo (v13) |
| Frozen frames in the body | ≈ 0 (median longest frozen run 2.5 f) |
| Main move: visible travel / total eased duration | ~5–6 f of travel inside a 30–37 f curve |
| Final hold (logo/sign-off) | 1.5–2.5 s, with slow drift or bloom breathing |

## Structure templates

**A. Product promo (8–12 s), the most common**
1. **Hook (0–1.5 s)**: a question or provocative line, or a 1–4 frame flash/glitch, then the line.
   Fast in: something moves on frame 0–2.
2. **Problem/context (1.5–3.5 s)**: show the situation with one UI element (search bar, chat, map).
3. **Turn (≈3.5 s)**: an interaction (cursor click, typing, a swipe) triggers a transformation (morph,
   portal, swoosh). **The fastest moment of the video is around 30–45% of the runtime.**
4. **Feature beats (3.5–8 s)**: 2–4 quick beats, 1–1.5 s each, each a morph of the previous.
5. **Resolve (last 2–3 s)**: the tagline small and calm → logo/brand lockup → hold.

**B. Loop reel (6–9 s, played twice)**: v05, v12, v14, v15, v23 repeat the same sequence twice before an
end card. Design the last frame to match the first (the logo shrinks into the starting element, or black to black).

**C. Long-form launch (25–40 s)**: v20, v09. Alternate "dense" sections (many small pops, chat bubbles
typing) and "calm" sections (2–3 s of one slow spinner or a statement). Pull back to show context every 3–4 s.

**D. Cinematic concept (12–16 s)**: v02. A slow mood opener (up to 5 s of glow + one element), then fast
UI beats, then a slow sign-off. Contrast between slow and fast is the point.

## When to go fast, when to slow down

Go **fast** (moves 5–8 f, cuts on beats, whips, flashes):
- the first second (hook) and the moment of transformation (the "aha");
- lists of examples (apps, files, avatars, platforms): one per 4–10 f;
- transitions between worlds (light↔dark, scene↔component);
- right before the logo (an energy spike, then calm).

Go **slow** (long eased moves, holds of 1–2.5 s with drift):
- when the viewer must **read** (≥ 0.25 s per word on screen + 0.5 s; a 5-word line needs ~1.7 s visible);
- product reveals (a device rising, a 3D turn): slow builds anticipation;
- mood/brand moments: glow openers, sign-offs, end cards;
- after a fast burst, so the next fast moment feels fast (contrast).

Rule of contrast: **never more than ~2 s of the same speed.** Alternate: fast burst → readable hold
(with drift) → fast burst. The velocity timeline of a good video looks like a series of sharp peaks
separated by gentle low plateaus that never reach zero.

## Music and beats

- Place cuts, flashes, slams and clicks **on beats**; place morph starts 2–3 f **before** the beat so the
  fastest frame lands on it.
- At 120 BPM a beat is 15 f; typical spacing of big events = 2 beats (30 f) or 4 beats (60 f).
- Word-by-word copy can be locked to beats (lyric-sync, v17) or to the voice-over (one word per spoken word, ±2 f).

## Readability budget

| Copy length on screen | Minimum visible time |
|---|---|
| 1–2 words | 12–20 f |
| 3–5 words | 35–50 f |
| 6–10 words (two lines) | 60–90 f |
| Logo + tagline | 45–75 f |
Type-on text counts from the moment the **last** word has appeared.
